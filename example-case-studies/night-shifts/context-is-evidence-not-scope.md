# Case Study: Context Is Evidence, Not Scope

> **Status: scope-discipline lesson for delegating post-mortems, reviews, and
> research tasks.**

## Executive Finding

After an overnight shift stopped early, the human owner requested a concise
lessons-learned case study on that shift and mentioned a second, adjacent
overnight session in passing as background context that might help spot shared
operational patterns.

When the LCA delegated the write-up, the delegate treated that background
mention as an instruction to conduct a full comparative investigation and
critique of the second session and alternative execution harnesses. That
unrequested side investigation displaced focus from the practical lessons the
requester actually wanted preserved.

## Expected vs Actual Behavior

| Element | Expected Behavior | Actual Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Primary source material | Incidents that directly sharpen actionable overnight-shift lessons | Mentioned background context was treated as a mandate to widen investigation scope |
| Use of adjacent session | Reference briefly *only* where it clarifies a reusable operational rule | Turned the adjacent session into an independent subject of audit and criticism |
| Analytical effort | Explain root causes and concrete playbook improvements | Spent tokens comparing sessions and harnesses with no durable payoff |
| Deliverable shape | Concise operational calibration | Sprawling comparative critique |

## Using Context With Judgment (No Artificial One-Incident Rule)

A human requester frequently mentions adjacent sessions, hypotheses, or other
repos to orient an agent's pattern recognition:

- **Do not impose a rigid one-incident rule:** one session can contain multiple
  instructive incidents, and combining related incidents is great whenever their
  interaction teaches a clearer lesson.
- **Do apply a practical payoff filter:** mentioning an adjacent event does
  *not* obligate the agent to audit it, assign blame to it, benchmark models or
  harnesses against it, or research tangential alternatives.

This is the exact prose analogue of requirements-risk bloat: once a side
comparison starts, every follow-on question inside it looks locally plausible
while adding zero value to the requested deliverable.

## Default Delegation Rule for Case Studies & Investigations

Include these three bounds in every investigation or post-mortem task packet:

1. **What durable lessons is this artifact meant to preserve?**
2. **Which specific incidents materially support those lessons?**
3. **Is an adjacent inquiry genuinely improving the lesson, or merely available
   in context?**

> **Bottom line:** Preserve the useful lessons. Include as many related
> incidents as genuinely clarify those lessons; leave out tangential side
> investigations and unrequested critiques that do not.
